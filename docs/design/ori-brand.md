# Ori Brand Direction

## Personality

Ori should feel:

- Intelligent
- Professional
- Human
- Technical
- Calm
- Precise
- Curious

## Visual direction

Ori should feel like a serious developer platform without becoming sterile or corporate. It should avoid generic AI-dashboard conventions such as excessive gradients, card grids, decorative activity indicators, and fake system metrics.

## Typography

The planned custom Ori family draws inspiration from two qualities:

- classic editorial character and serif structure
- high readability, open forms, and comfortable spacing

The resulting glyphs must be original. The goal is to capture qualities, not copy another typeface.

## Planned type roles

### Ori Display
Branding, large headings, and major product moments.

### Ori Text
Chat, navigation, settings, documentation, and normal UI.

### Ori Mono
Code, terminal output, logs, identifiers, and technical values.

## Color philosophy

Color is semantic and restrained. It should communicate interaction, state, hierarchy, and feedback rather than decorate every surface.

Status should never depend on color alone.

## Surface philosophy

Ori avoids card soup. Containers exist to group information or establish interaction context. The default visual structure is background → content → sections → contextual surfaces.

## Motion philosophy

Motion should communicate state, cause/effect, and transitions. It should be subtle and should not distract from work.
